7.1Why balance

A mass m whose centre is a radius r from the axis of a shaft turning at ω pulls outwards with the centrifugal force FC = m ω² r. The force turns with the shaft, so each bearing feels a load that goes round once per revolution: it bends the shaft, loads the bearings and shakes the frame. Because the force grows with ω², a small error matters a lot at speed. Balancing means adding masses so that the centrifugal forces, and their moments, cancel.

An unbalanced disc on a shaft

Bearing pedestals move in proportion to the force on them (much exaggerated). Arrows: force on each bearing. Drag to turn the view.

The four cases in the notes build on each other: (1) one mass balanced by one mass in the same plane; (2) one mass balanced by two masses in other planes; (3) several masses in one plane; (4) several masses in several planes.

7.2One mass balanced by one mass in the same plane

Put the balancing mass m2 opposite the disturbing mass m1, in the same plane, so the two centrifugal forces are equal and opposite. Since ω is the same for both, only the products matter:

m1​ω2r1​=m2​ω2r2​⇒m1​r1​=m2​r2​
The product m2r2 can be split any way; a large radius keeps the balancing mass small.

7.3One mass balanced by two masses in different planes

A single balancing mass in another plane cancels the force but leaves a couple that rocks the shaft in its bearings. Two balancing masses in planes L and M can cancel both. With l the distance between L and M, l1 from A to L and l2 from A to M:

A between L and M:mr=m1​r1​+m2​r2​,m1​r1​=lmrl2​​,m2​r2​=lmrl1​​A outside L and M:mr+m2​r2​=m1​r1​
Between the planes both balancing masses are opposite to m. Outside them, one is opposite to m and one is on the same side, and the moment equations are the same. Force balance alone is static balance; force and moment balance together is dynamic balance.

7.4Several masses rotating in the same plane

Analytical method. Resolve every m r horizontally and vertically, add, find the resultant, and put the balancing mass opposite it:

ΣH=Σmrcosθ,ΣV=Σmrsinθ,R=ΣH2+ΣV2​,tanθ′=ΣHΣV​balancing mass:mr=R  at θ′+180∘

Graphical method. Draw the vectors m r head to tail (ab, bc, cd, de). The closing side ae is the resultant; the balancing vector is ea, the same length pointing the other way. Drag the masses in the space diagram or edit the table.

7.5Several masses rotating in different planes

Move every mass to a reference plane (R.P.), here plane L. Each move leaves its force m r in the R.P. plus a couple m r l, where l is the distance from the R.P. (negative to the left). For complete balance both must vanish:

  1. The couples must balance: draw the couple polygon of the m r l vectors (outward for masses on one side of the R.P., inward on the other). Its closing side is the couple the mass in plane M must supply: mM rM lM.
  2. The forces must balance: draw the force polygon of the m r values, now including mM rM. Its closing side is mL rL.
