7.1Governors

What a governor does

  • A governor keeps the mean speed of an engine within limits as the load changes, by adjusting the fuel, steam or water supply. It is the simplest automatic control: speed is measured, compared and corrected in one device.
  • A flywheel (Chapter 2) limits the speed fluctuation within a cycle; it cannot respond to a lasting change of load. A governor responds over many cycles to the change of load.
  • Examples: the diesel generator set at a health centre must hold 1500 rpm (50 Hz) whether one bulb or the theatre lights are on; a mill engine must not race when the grain runs out; a small hydro turbine must not run away when the line trips.

Primary reading: Rattan (2009), chapters 16 and 17.

Types of governor

  • Centrifugal governors: rotating balls fly out as the speed rises and lift a sleeve that works the throttle.
    • Pendulum type: Watt.
    • Gravity (dead-weight) loaded: Porter, Proell.
    • Spring loaded: Hartnell, Hartung, Wilson-Hartnell, Pickering.
  • Inertia governors: respond to the rate of change of speed, so they act sooner. Less common.
  • Modern engines use electronic governors (a speed sensor, a controller and an actuator), but the same ideas of stability, sensitiveness and hunting apply.

7.2Watt and Porter governors

The Watt governor

Two balls of mass mmm on arms hinged at the spindle axis, at radius rrr and height hhh. Neglecting the arm and sleeve masses, take moments about the hinge O:

The Porter governor

A heavy central load (sleeve) of mass MMM rides on the lower arms. With q=tan⁡β/tan⁡αq = \tan\beta/\tan\alphaq=tanβ/tanα, the ratio of the lower-arm to upper-arm angles' tangents, moments about the instantaneous centre of the lower arm give

The Proell governor

  • Like a Porter governor, but the balls sit on upward extensions of the lower arms (point E), not at the arm joint (point B).
  • Taking moments about the instantaneous centre with the ball at E gives N2=FMBM⋅895h⋅m+M2(1+q)mN^2 = \dfrac{FM}{BM}\cdot\dfrac{895}{h}\cdot\dfrac{m + \tfrac{M}{2}(1 + q)}{m}N2=BMFM​⋅h895​⋅mm+2M​(1+q)​, where FM/BMFM/BMFM/BM is a geometric ratio greater than 1.
  • So a Proell governor runs faster than a Porter governor of the same masses and height, or can use smaller balls for the same speed.

7.3Spring-loaded governors

The Hartnell governor

  • Bell-crank levers pivoted on the rotating frame carry the balls on their vertical arms (length aaa) and push the sleeve with their horizontal arms (length bbb).
  • A compressed spring on the spindle presses down on the sleeve with force SSS. Springs give large forces in a small space, so Hartnell governors run fast, at any angle, even on a moving vehicle.

Neglecting the ball weight, obliquity and friction, moments about each lever pivot:

mω2r⋅a=Mg+S2⋅b⇒Mg+S=2mω2r abm\omega^2 r\cdot a = \frac{Mg + S}{2}\cdot b \quad\Rightarrow\quad Mg + S = 2m\omega^2 r\,\frac{a}{b}mω2r⋅a=2Mg+S​⋅b⇒Mg+S=2mω2rba​

Controlling force, stability and isochronism

  • The controlling force FFF is the inward force on each ball needed for equilibrium; at equilibrium speed F=mω2rF = m\omega^2 rF=mω2r. Plotting FFF against rrr gives the governor's characteristic.
  • For a spring governor it is close to a straight line F=αr+βF = \alpha r + \betaF=αr+β.
  • β<0\beta < 0β<0 (line cuts the force axis below the origin): stable, since speed rises with radius.
  • β=0\beta = 0β=0 (line through the origin): isochronous, since F/r=mω2F/r = m\omega^2F/r=mω2 is the same at every radius.
  • β>0\beta > 0β>0: unstable, since the speed falls as the balls fly out, and the governor runs to its stops.

Sensitiveness, hunting, effort and power

  • A sensitive governor moves a long way for a small speed change. Taken too far (isochronous), the sleeve flies between its stops at the slightest change, the fuel swings from full to none, and the speed hunts above and below the mean. A practical governor trades some sensitiveness for stability.
  • Effort: the mean force exerted at the sleeve for a given fractional change of speed ccc. For a Porter governor with q=1q = 1q=1, E=c (m+M) gE = c\,(m + M)\,gE=c(m+M)g.
  • Power: the work done at the sleeve for that change, effort × sleeve lift. With q=1q = 1q=1 the sleeve sits at 2h2h2h below the top pivot, and hhh varies as 1/ω21/\omega^21/ω2, so the lift is x=2h[1−1/(1+c)2]x = 2h\left[1 - 1/(1 + c)^2\right]x=2h[1−1/(1+c)2].
  • The governor must have enough effort and power to overcome the friction of the throttle linkage. That is why loaded governors replaced the Watt governor.

7.4Gyroscopic couple

Angular momentum and precession

  • A rotor of moment of inertia III spinning at ω\omegaω has angular momentum H=IωH = I\omegaH=Iω, a vector along the spin axis by the right-hand rule.
  • If the spin axis is turned (precessed) at ωp\omega_pωp​ about a perpendicular axis, the direction of HHH changes. In time δt\delta tδt the change is Iω δθI\omega\,\delta\thetaIωδθ, at right angles to HHH.
  • A rate of change of angular momentum needs a couple:
C=Iωdθdt=Iω ωpC = I\omega\frac{d\theta}{dt} = I\omega\,\omega_pC=Iωdtdθ​=Iωωp​gyroscopic couple

Applied and reaction couples

  • The applied (active) couple is the one that must act on the rotor to precess it: its vector is ω⃗p×H⃗\vec\omega_p \times \vec Hωp​×H.
  • The rotor pushes back on its bearings, and so on the vehicle, with the equal and opposite reaction couple. That is the "gyroscopic effect" felt by an aircraft, ship or vehicle.
  • Rule: turn the spin vector through 90° in the sense of the precession. That gives the direction of the applied couple vector; the reaction couple is the reverse.
  • If the precession axis is parallel to the spin axis (a ship rolling about the axis of a fore-and-aft turbine), there is no gyroscopic couple.

7.5Gyroscopic effects on aircraft, ships and vehicles

Aeroplanes

  • The engine and propeller spin about the fore-and-aft axis. A turn precesses them about the vertical axis at ωp=v/R\omega_p = v/Rωp​=v/R.
  • With the propeller turning clockwise as seen from the rear: a left turn raises the nose and depresses the tail; a right turn depresses the nose and raises the tail.
  • With the propeller turning the other way, every effect reverses. Pilots of single-engine aircraft feel this as a pitch kick in sharp turns.
Example 7.5 (Rattan, 2009, Example 17.3)An aeroplane flying at 240 km/h turns left through a quarter circle of 60 m radius. The engine and propeller have a mass of 450 kg and radius of gyration 320 mm, and turn at 2000 rpm clockwise seen from the rear. Find the gyroscopic couple and its effect.Show solution
  1. I=450×0.322=46.08I = 450 \times 0.32^2 = 46.08I=450×0.322=46.08 kg m²; ω=209.4\omega = 209.4ω=209.4 rad/s; v=66.67v = 66.67v=66.67 m/s; ωp=66.67/60=1.111\omega_p = 66.67/60 = 1.111ωp​=66.67/60=1.111 rad/s.
  2. C=46.08×209.4×1.111=10 720C = 46.08 \times 209.4 \times 1.111 = 10\,720C=46.08×209.4×1.111=10720 N m.

About 10.7 kN m, raising the nose and depressing the tail. A right turn would depress the nose.

Ships: steering, pitching and rolling

  • Steering (turning): like the aeroplane. Rotor clockwise seen from the stern: steering left raises the bow and lowers the stern; steering right lowers the bow.
  • Pitching (bow rising and falling, roughly simple harmonic): the precession is about the athwartships axis and the reaction makes the ship yaw. Rotor clockwise from the stern and bow rising: the ship turns to starboard (right); bow falling: to port. For pitching of amplitude ϕ\phiϕ and period tpt_ptp​, the greatest precession is ωp,max=ϕ⋅2π/tp\omega_{p,max} = \phi\cdot 2\pi/t_pωp,max​=ϕ⋅2π/tp​.
  • Rolling: the precession axis is parallel to the rotor axis, so there is no gyroscopic effect.

Stability of a four-wheeled vehicle on a bend

On a bend of radius RRR at speed vvv, the reactions on the wheels change in three ways. The outer wheels gain and the inner wheels lose load; if an inner reaction reaches zero, the vehicle starts to overturn.

  • Weight: mg/4mg/4mg/4 up on each wheel.
  • Gyroscopic couple of the four road wheels and the engine: Cg=(4Iw+GIe)v2rwRC_g = \left(4I_w + G I_e\right)\dfrac{v^2}{r_w R}Cg​=(4Iw​+GIe​)rw​Rv2​ (engine turning the same way as the wheels, gear ratio GGG), shared as ±Cg/2w\pm C_g/2w±Cg​/2w between the outer and inner wheels (track www).

  • A motorcycle on a bend leans inwards at angle θ\thetaθ. The wheels' and engine's gyroscopic couple and the centrifugal couple both try to overturn it outwards; leaning in lets the weight balance them.
